I analyzed candy sales data using SQL and Tableau to understand product performance, profits, and purchasing behavior. The goal was to identify trends and explore how businesses can use data to make more informed decisions.

01 / THE QUESTION

What makes a product worth selling?

Just because a product sells well doesn't necessarily mean it's the most profitable. I wanted to understand which products actually bring in the most profit, how customers' purchasing habits differ, and which states generate the most revenue.

For this project, I used SQL to analyze a candy distributor's sales data and answer questions that could help a business make decisions about its products/customers. I then used Tableau to make visualizations of my findings.

The SQL

I used SQL to answer five questions about the company's sales, products, and customers. Open any query to inspect the logic.

QUESTION 01 / SUM, GROUP BY, ORDER BY

Division performance

Which candy divisions generate the most revenue, and are they also the most profitable?

Finding. In the sample Chocolate generated about 92% of revenue and Sugar had the highest division gross profit margin at 70.90%.

Explore the SQL query ↗
SELECT
    Division,
    SUM(Sales) AS "Total Revenue",
    SUM(Gross_Profit) AS "Total Gross Profit",
    (SUM(Gross_Profit) * 100.0 / SUM(Sales)) AS "Gross Profit Margin (%)"
FROM Candy_Sales
GROUP BY Division
ORDER BY "Total Revenue" DESC
QUESTION 02 / INNER JOIN, SUM, calculated margins

Product performance

Which products generate the most sales and gross profit?

Finding. Scrumdiddlyumptious had the highest revenue ($3,571.20). Nutty Crunch Surprise had $2,753.61 in revenue with a 71.35% margin. Everlasting Gobstopper had an 80% margin on just $60 revenue.

Explore the SQL query ↗
SELECT
    Product_Name,
    SUM(Sales) AS "Total Revenue",
    SUM(Gross_Profit) AS "Total Gross Profit",
    SUM(Units) AS "Units Sold",
    (SUM(Gross_Profit) * 100.0 / SUM(Sales)) AS "Gross Profit Margin (%)"
FROM Candy_Sales
INNER JOIN Candy_Products
    ON Candy_Sales.Product_ID = Candy_Products.Product_ID
GROUP BY Product_Name
ORDER BY "Total Revenue" DESC
QUESTION 03 / COUNT(DISTINCT), SUM, GROUP BY

Customer value

Which customers contribute the most revenue and how does the number of orders a customer places relate to how much they spend?

Finding. Customer 165344 spent $153 across two orders, while other customers had more orders but lower revenue. Frequency alone was not enough to identify the highest spending customer.

Explore the SQL query ↗
SELECT
    Customer_ID,
    COUNT(DISTINCT Order_ID) AS "Number of Orders",
    SUM(Sales) AS "Total Revenue",
    SUM(Gross_Profit) AS "Total Gross Profit"
FROM Candy_Sales
GROUP BY Customer_ID
ORDER BY "Total Revenue" DESC
QUESTION 04 / Subquery, AVG, COUNT(DISTINCT)

Customer spending patterns

How does average observed customer revenue differ between single order and multiple order customers?

Finding. 959 single-order customers averaged $15.93 in total customer revenue and 72 multiple-order customers averaged $35.27. This is total customer revenue and not revenue per order.

Explore the SQL query ↗
-- Change the comparison to = 1 for single-order customers,
-- or > 1 for customers with multiple recorded orders.
SELECT
    AVG(Total_Revenue) AS "Average Customer Revenue"
FROM (
    SELECT
        Customer_ID,
        COUNT(DISTINCT Order_ID) AS Number_of_Orders,
        SUM(Sales) AS Total_Revenue
    FROM Candy_Sales
    GROUP BY Customer_ID
) AS Customer_Summary
WHERE Number_of_Orders > 1
QUESTION 05 / WHERE, GROUP BY, ORDER BY

Geographic performance

Which U.S. states generate the most revenue and gross profit?

Finding. California generated $3,920.60 in revenue, then New York at $1,875.90 and Texas at $1,674.97.

Explore the SQL query ↗
SELECT
    State,
    SUM(Sales) AS "Total Revenue",
    SUM(Gross_Profit) AS "Total Gross Profit"
FROM Candy_Sales
WHERE Country = 'United States'
GROUP BY State
ORDER BY "Total Revenue" DESC

05 / WHAT THE NUMBERS SUGGEST

Findings, not
just charts.

92%

Chocolate leads the business mix.

Chocolate accounted for approximately 92% of sample revenue. It contributed the most total gross profit, while the Sugar division had a slightly higher gross profit margin. That tension suggests evaluating both volume and efficiency.

71.35%

A promising product to investigate.

Nutty Crunch Surprise had a higher margin than the top-revenue product, Scrumdiddlyumptious, while still generating meaningful revenue. A logical next step would be to test demand and inventory constraints.

2.21×

More orders, more total customer revenue.

Customers with multiple orders captured in the sample generated 2.21 times the average total customer revenue of single-order customers. This is not evidence that they spent more per order or that retention caused the difference.

POTENTIAL BUSINESS ACTIONS

Protect availability for strong-selling products; investigate high-margin products with room for demand growth; examine customer retention alongside average order value; and review regional coverage before recommending investment in lower-sales states.

06 / WHAT I LEARNED

When zero
wasn't zero.

My first profitability visualization reported a 0% margin for several products despite positive gross profit. I compared the chart back to my SQL output and found the issue: integer division in SQLite truncated the fraction before multiplying it by 100.

INITIAL CALCULATION(SUM(Gross_Profit) / SUM(Sales)) * 100
CORRECTED CALCULATIONSUM(Gross_Profit) * 100.0 / SUM(Sales)

Adding 100.0 kept the calculation in floating-point arithmetic. The experience reinforced that a polished visualization is only as good as its underlying calculations—and that unexpected values deserve investigation.

THE TAKEAWAY

Good analysis explains why.

SQL helped me build and test the questions; Tableau helped make the findings explorable. The most useful step was connecting the outputs to decisions while being clear about what a sample can—and cannot—tell us.
